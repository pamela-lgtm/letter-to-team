Where we are, and where we are going

A Letter to the Team

From Pamela Syvertson · Owner & Broker

I want to tell you directly where I am, what I am doing, and what I am asking of you — because you deserve to hear it from me rather than infer it from changes you notice along the way.

I am back at the helm, and I am staying there

For a period of time I handed the wheel to others. No one did anything wrong. But this is my company, my license, my name on the door, and the standard that carries it is mine to set and mine to defend. I stepped back from that. I am not going to do it again.

That is not a criticism of anyone. It is a correction I am making about myself, and it changes things in a way you will feel almost immediately.

I am the one selling our service now

Every new owner who comes into this portfolio comes through me. I interview them. I decide. And I want you to understand exactly what I am promising on your behalf when I sit across from someone, because those promises become your work.

I am telling them:

  • That we communicate before they have to ask
  • That they will never be surprised by something we already knew
  • That we tell them the truth even when the truth is inconvenient for us
  • That we treat their property as the asset it is, and protect it accordingly
  • That we cannot guarantee a perfect outcome — but that with these things in place, their odds are dramatically better than they would be anywhere else

I take that seriously. I am not comfortable promising something I am not certain we deliver, which is why this letter exists.

We do not take every owner, and that is for you

I turn people away. I decline properties that are not show-ready. I decline owners who are not genuinely aligned with maintaining their investment. I do this in slow months as well as busy ones, and I will keep doing it.

I want to be clear about why, because from the outside it can look like being particular for its own sake. It is not.

Every misaligned owner we accept is paid for by this team. In arguments about repairs. In fires that never had to start. In hours that should have gone to owners who say yes.

Selectivity is not a marketing position. It is what makes the level of service I am promising physically possible for a team this size to deliver. When I decline an owner, I am protecting your ability to do excellent work for the ones we have.

It also changes who we work with. Owners who meet a high standard expect one — and they are, without exception, better to work with than owners who do not.

What we have built, and what comes next

We have spent real time rebuilding our systems and reinforcing our standards. That work is not decoration. It is the foundation that makes consistency possible, and we now have one.

We are not finished. We will keep refining, and here is my commitment to you as we do:

What I am responsible for
  • You will not be asked to deliver a promise without the tools to deliver it. Megan and Abril have taken on an enormous undertaking rebuilding our processes with Athenee, and I am in it alongside them helping refine. Making sure that framework exists before I sell against it is my responsibility.
  • I will tell you why, not just what. Every standard I set, you will understand the reasoning behind. You are not executing instructions; you are running a business with me.
  • I will communicate with you regularly — not only when something is wrong.
  • I will keep the gate closed. A slow month is not a reason to accept an owner who will be difficult for as long as we have them.

What I'm Asking of You

Consistency.

Not effort — I have never doubted the effort or commitment of anyone on this team. What I am asking for is consistency.

We already have a talented and capable team. The difference between good property management and exceptional property management is whether our standards are upheld consistently — on an ordinary Tuesday, when no one is watching, nothing is urgent, and there is no crisis demanding our attention.

Excellence is not simply how we respond when something goes wrong. It is the standard we maintain every day, especially when nothing is on fire.

That requires proactivity — always, not occasionally.

Proactivity means anticipating the next step, following through without being reminded, communicating before someone has to ask, and taking ownership until the matter is fully resolved.

A standard applied only sometimes is not truly a standard; it is a preference. Our clients may not always be able to identify exactly where the difference lies, but they can feel it in their experience.

Consistency is what creates trust. Proactivity is what makes our clients feel cared for. Together, they are what will distinguish Verandah Properties as exceptional.

What this buys us

I want to be honest that I am asking for something hard. So let me tell you what it produces, because it is not abstract and it is not only for the clients.

Almost every fire we fight is the delayed cost of something that was not done consistently earlier. Angry owners are rarely angry about the event itself. They are angry about the silence that preceded it. Reactive work is exhausting in a way that proactive work simply is not — because reactive work is always someone else's emergency arriving on your desk with no warning and no good options.

When we are one step ahead, the work stops being about damage control and starts being about value. And that work earns praise instead of blame.

That is the trade I am asking you to make with me. More discipline now, in exchange for far fewer emergencies later — and time freed up to do the kind of work that makes people grateful to have us rather than relieved we finally responded.

Why this matters beyond us

This industry has a poor reputation, and it earned it. Owners expect to be ignored. Residents expect to be treated as an inconvenience. The bar is genuinely low.

That is our opportunity. Being excellent where mediocrity is normal is the strongest position in business — and it is the most satisfying place to work. Our owners are not buying a service. They are protecting their families' financial future against money that loses value every year, using an asset that houses people and always will. What we do with that responsibility matters.

I want you proud of where you work. Not because I said something motivating in a letter, but because you know what we deliver and you know most firms do not.

One more thing

There are two kinds of people in any business. There are those who advocate for themselves in the short term. And there are those who work honestly, in service of others, and are rewarded in a way that is quieter but far more worth having.

I have built this company entirely on the second, over twenty-five years, and I have never regretted it. That is who I hire. That is who I keep. That is who I intend to reward — and I am working on exactly how, because I do not think anyone should carry a standard this high without sharing in what it produces.

This letter is the first of many. I am going to keep building this out with you, piece by piece, so that the philosophy behind what we do is as clear to you as the tasks are.

Thank you for being here. I mean that.

Pamela
Pamela Syvertson · Owner & Broker
